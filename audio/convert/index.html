What it does

Decodes your file and re-encodes it in the format you choose using the FFmpeg.wasm engine that runs on your device. Lossy formats expose a bitrate control; lossless formats keep every sample. The result is validated, played back for comparison and offered as a download with its true byte size.

How to use

  1. Add an MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG, Opus, AIFF, AMR or WMA file. Bench decodes it and shows duration, sample rate and channels.
  2. Pick the output format. Choose a bitrate for lossy formats or a lower sample rate to save space.
  3. Run the converter. The engine is downloaded the first time and cached afterwards.
  4. Preview the result and download it. If the requested format is unavailable, Bench says so instead of faking the output.

Common use cases

  • Turn a WAV recording into a small MP3.
  • Convert an M4A voice memo to WAV for editing.
  • Produce a FLAC master from a lossless source.

Limits & considerations

MP3 output uses libmp3lame and AAC uses FFmpeg's encoder; both are lossy and re-encoding lossy audio again can add artefacts. Very long files are bounded by the 30-minute / 100 MB limits. The first conversion downloads the ~31 MB engine.

How your audio is processed

This tool encodes with the FFmpeg engine running as WebAssembly inside your browser. The engine is downloaded once (about 31 MB) and cached; the audio is processed locally and is never uploaded.

Privacy & security

Decoding, editing and encoding run inside your browser. Files are read from local memory into temporary object URLs and are never uploaded, logged or stored by Bench. The FFmpeg.wasm engine is downloaded once from this site (same origin) and runs entirely on your device.

Frequently asked questions

Which formats can I convert between?

MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC/M4A, OGG Vorbis, Opus and AIFF in any direction the encoder supports, plus decoding of AMR, WMA, Speex and more.

Why is the first conversion slow?

The FFmpeg.wasm engine (~31 MB) is downloaded once and cached by your browser. Later conversions start instantly.

Does converting lose quality?

Converting to WAV, FLAC or AIFF is lossless. Converting to MP3, AAC, OGG or Opus is lossy; converting lossy to lossy can add artefacts, so start from the best source you have.
