About this tool — how it works, limits & FAQ

What it does

Validates, cleans, and standardizes podcast and YouTube video chapter markers. Ensures timestamps are sorted chronologically, correctly padded (MM:SS or HH:MM:SS), separated by valid delimiters, and compliant with platform chapter guidelines.

Common use cases

  • YouTube Video Chapter Timestamps: Format raw cue points into YouTube description syntax (e.g., 00:00 Introduction) for automatic video progress bar chapters.
  • Podcast Show Notes Formatting: Generate clean timestamped episode summaries for Spotify, Apple Podcasts, and RSS feed show notes.
  • Audiobook Track Indexing: Convert spoken audio markers into standardized chapter timestamps for digital audio distributors.

How to use

  1. Paste your raw timestamp notes and chapter titles into the multiline text input box.
  2. Verify the syntax to ensure the first chapter begins precisely at 00:00 as required by platforms.
  3. Click the Download Chapters button to receive a formatted chapters.txt file.
  4. Paste the cleaned output directly into your YouTube description, Spotify show notes, or podcast RSS feed.

Limits & considerations

Formats and validates plain text timecode strings. Does not embed ID3v2 chapter frames directly into binary MP3 or M4A audio containers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the mandatory starting requirement for YouTube video chapters?

YouTube requires the very first chapter timestamp to begin at exactly 00:00, have at least three chapters, and maintain a minimum of 10 seconds per chapter.

Can I paste raw seconds or decimal timestamps to convert them?

Yes. The parser recognizes raw second counts, MM:SS, and HH:MM:SS formats, converting them into standard chapter formatting.

Can I export the formatted chapters directly to my clipboard?

Yes. A single-click copy button allows you to instantly transfer formatted chapters directly into YouTube Studio or your podcast host.

Privacy & security

Text formatting is executed in local browser JavaScript. Your unreleased episode titles and show notes remain 100% private on your device.
