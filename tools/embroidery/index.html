About this tool — how it works, limits & FAQ

What it does

A dedicated colour-reduction studio rather than a stitch chart. Your image is average-pooled, clustered with median-cut quantisation to any thread count from 2 to 64, and either left as exact clustered colours or snapped to the shared DMC floss palette. You can also paste your own hex palette (one colour per line) to match non-DMC threads Ã¢â‚¬â€ ideal for planning from a specific thread set or a fabric swatch. The preview shows the original photograph beside a smooth, non-gridded simplified render so you can judge the reduction before committing, and the legend lists every thread's code or hex, name, swatch, share of the design as a percentage and an estimated skein count (~1,700 stitches per skein). Common use cases

  • Hand Embroidery Pattern Tracing: Simplify complex illustrations into flat color blocks and crisp boundary lines for transfer to linen or cotton fabric.

Limits & considerations

Designed for hand-embroidery colour planning and floss selection; it does not generate machine-embroidery files such as DST, PES or JEF, and it does not auto-trace stitch direction or shading. Custom palettes accept up to 64 hex colours. Frequently asked questions

How does the pattern simplifier differ from the cross-stitch tool?

While cross-stitch creates a rigid pixelated grid, the embroidery simplifier reduces color gradients into continuous organic color regions suitable for satin and fill stitches.

Does the tool support dithering to blend color transitions?

Yes. You can toggle error-diffusion dithering on or off depending on whether you prefer smooth color blending or hard, clean stitch boundaries.

How can I transfer the finished design onto fabric?

Export a high-contrast black-and-white line version or color guide, print it at 100% scale, and transfer using water-soluble stabilizer or heat-transfer pens.
