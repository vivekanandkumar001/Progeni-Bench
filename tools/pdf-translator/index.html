About this tool — how it works, limits & FAQ

What it does

Extracts text in your browser, translates it entirely on your device on Chrome 138+ (built-in on-device Translator) or via a server endpoint as fallback, and assembles a downloadable PDF locally. It supports best-effort layout reconstruction, clean readable pages, bilingual source-and-translation pages, page ranges, and local OCR for image-only pages.

Common use cases

  • Translate text-based reports, guides and study materials while retaining their page structure where practical.
  • Convert Hindi translations to Roman script for a best-effort Hinglish reading copy.
  • OCR scanned English or Hindi pages locally before translating selected pages.

How to use

  1. Choose a PDF under 15 MB and review its page count.
  2. Select a target language, translation style and output layout; optionally set a source language and page range.
  3. Enable local OCR for scanned pages. OCR requires downloading language data to your browser, but page images stay on your device.
  4. Translate and review the text preview and page-quality report before downloading the generated PDF.

Limits & considerations

Maximum 15 MB and 120,000 extracted characters per job. Complex tables, embedded image text, handwriting, unusual fonts and expanded translations can affect layout. Original-layout reconstruction is best-effort, links are not preserved as interactive links, and output pages are rasterized. Hinglish is Hindi translated then romanized; the mix of Hindi and English is not guaranteed. The on-device translator processes text locally; otherwise text goes to a server translation endpoint.

Frequently asked questions

Is my PDF uploaded to Bench?

No. PDF pages stay in your browser. On Chrome 138+, even extracted text never leaves your device — it is translated entirely on your device using the built-in on-device Translator.

Does Hinglish use Roman script?

Yes. The tool translates to Hindi and romanizes that output. This is best-effort Romanized Hindi, not guaranteed natural Hindi-English mixing.

Can scanned documents be translated?

Yes, when local OCR is enabled and the required language data is available. OCR may be incomplete for low-quality scans, handwriting and image-heavy pages.

Privacy & security

The PDF itself is parsed, rendered, OCR-processed and rebuilt locally in your browser. On Chrome 138+, translation runs entirely on your device — no text is sent anywhere. Otherwise extracted text is translated through a provider configured server-side (DeepL when set, or an anonymous fallback). OCR language data is downloaded from the Tesseract data host, but document images are not sent there.
