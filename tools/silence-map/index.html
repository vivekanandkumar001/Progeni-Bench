Podcast Silence Map

Detect dead air with a dBFS threshold slider, waveform view and CSV/TXT gap export. Zero server uploads — all processing happens directly in your browser.

What it does

Decodes an audio track locally and scans its energy window-by-window to map every silence gap longer than a length you choose. Set the silence threshold as a dBFS slider (converted to amplitude), the analysis window size, the minimum gap length and the channel mode (left, right, mono mix or max-of-channels). The peak-envelope waveform shades each detected gap, and clicking a gap preview-plays just that stretch through an embedded player. Reports file duration, total silence, percentage silence and gap count, listing every gap as HH:MM:SS.mmm — including recordings longer than an hour.

Common use cases

  • Podcast Editing Audits: Locate long pauses, dead air, and unedited speaker gaps in raw audio tracks before manual editing.
  • Voiceover Pacing Review: Visualize cadence, silence duration, and sentence rhythm across audiobook and voice recording sessions.
  • Interview Audio Quality Check: Identify potential audio dropout issues or microphone mute accidents in recorded conversations.

How to use

  1. Drop a podcast audio track (WAV, MP3, AAC or OGG) into the silence detector.
  2. Set the silence threshold in dBFS, the window size, the minimum gap length and the channel mode for your recording.
  3. Review the shaded waveform and the gap table, and click any row to preview-play that stretch of audio.
  4. Download the gaps as CSV (Start, End, Duration) or a plain-text list, or copy the list to the clipboard.

Limits & Considerations

Decodes audio into browser RAM; extremely large audio files (>200 MB) may hit memory limits on constrained devices. Undecodable codecs (for example DRM-protected M4A, unsupported FLAC builds or raw AC-3) show codec guidance instead of results — convert to WAV or standard MP3 and retry.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is audio decoded without uploading large sound files?

Your browser's built-in Web Audio API decodes the audio waveform directly in RAM, calculating Root Mean Square (RMS) decibel levels locally.

Can I adjust the silence threshold sensitivity?

Yes. You can customize the decibel cutoff (e.g. -40 dB or -50 dB) and minimum silence duration to distinguish natural breathing from dead air.

What audio formats can I inspect?

Standard browser-supported audio formats including MP3, WAV, AAC, M4A, and OGG can be analyzed directly.

Privacy & Security

Audio decoding, waveform inspection, and silence mapping execute entirely on your local CPU. Unreleased audio files never leave your computer.
