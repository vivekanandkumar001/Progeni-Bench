What it does

Rebalances and rewraps overlong SRT subtitle lines with a two-pass algorithm: it first splits at the clause punctuation nearest the midpoint for a natural break, then greedily wraps the remaining words to your character-per-line limit. Tokens that exceed the limit on their own are hard-cut, and inline styling tags such as and {\an8} are preserved as anchored spans so formatting survives the rewrap. Adjust the maximum characters per line (20-60), the maximum number of lines (1-3) and tag preservation, then compare a diff-style before/after preview with a changed-cue count. Timecodes are never touched, and you can download the fixed .srt or send it back to the speed checker.

Common use cases

  • Natural Syntactic Clause Rewrapping: Rebalance awkward multi-line subtitles so line breaks occur at natural linguistic commas and clause boundaries.
  • Maximum Line Width Enforcement: Ensure no subtitle line exceeds character limits (such as 37 or 42 characters per line) for broadcast and streaming standards.
  • Subtitle Duration & Tag Preservation: Reformat text layout while preserving exact millisecond start and end timecodes and italic styling tags.

How to use

  1. Drop your wide or single-line .srt subtitle file into the line-break fixer, or click Load sample.
  2. Set the maximum characters per line, the maximum number of lines and whether to preserve inline tags.
  3. Review the diff-style preview, which shows the changed-cue count and the before/after line breaks for each affected cue.
  4. Download the fixed .srt subtitle file, or click Send to speed checker to audit the repaired file for reading speed.

Limits & Considerations

Rewraps SubRip SRT subtitle text only. It does not alter timecodes, merge or split cues, or translate subtitle language.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will rewrapping subtitle lines change my audio synchronization?

No. The line-break fixer modifies only text formatting and line divisions; all original cue timecodes remain unaltered.

How does the tool decide where to split a long subtitle line?

The algorithm evaluates clause boundaries and punctuation marks near the midpoint of the sentence rather than making arbitrary word wraps.

Does the tool support standard SRT subtitle files?

Yes. It accepts SubRip (.srt) files with CRLF or LF line endings and exports compliant SRT files with clean line formatting.

Privacy & Security

Subtitle files are parsed purely within client-side memory. Confidential movie scripts, documentary translations, and transcripts remain strictly private.
