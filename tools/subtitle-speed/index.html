What it does

Audits SubRip (.srt) subtitle files against broadcast reading-speed standards. Choose the Netflix profile (21 CPS, 1s minimum, 42 characters), the BBC profile (about 180 words per minute, 16-20 CPS), or a custom profile with your own CPS, minimum-duration and line-length limits. The engine strips styling tags before measuring, reports raw and clean character counts, and flags excessive CPS, short or zero-duration cues, overlong lines and overlapping timecodes (naming the neighbouring cue and the clashing range). It shows totals, flagged counts, an average CPS and the longest cue, and exports a CSV report or an annotated .issues.srt review copy.

Common use cases

  • Streaming Accessibility Compliance: Audit SRT subtitle files against industry reading speed standards such as Netflix's 20 CPS or BBC's 17 CPS rules.
  • Language Learning Caption Pacing: Ensure subtitles for non-native speakers maintain comfortable reading speeds without visual flashing.
  • Video Caption QC: Flag subtitles with zero durations, negative timecodes, or overlapping speech blocks before final video burning.

How to use

  1. Upload your SubRip (.srt) subtitle caption file, or click Load sample to see the checker in action.
  2. Pick a reading-speed profile (Netflix, BBC or Custom) and tweak the CPS, minimum duration and character limits if you need to.
  3. Review the stats summary and the issue table, where each flagged cue lists its type, raw/clean character counts and details such as the overlapping neighbour and time range.
  4. Download the CSV report for a spreadsheet, the annotated .issues.srt for review, or click Fix line breaks to send the text straight to the line-break fixer.

Limits & Considerations

Audits SubRip SRT subtitle files. Does not directly extract proprietary binary subtitle streams embedded in container formats like MKV or MP4.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Characters Per Second (CPS) and why is it important?

CPS measures how quickly a viewer must read on-screen text. Rates exceeding 20-25 CPS often result in missed dialogue or viewer fatigue.

Does the checker report overlapping subtitle cues?

Yes. The audit detects cue timecode collisions, highlighting cues where the start time precedes the previous cue's end time.

Can I export an audit report of flagged subtitle issues?

Yes. You can export a detailed CSV audit report listing line numbers, text, CPS values, and timing violations.

Privacy & Security

Subtitle files are parsed purely within client-side memory. Confidential movie scripts, documentary translations, and transcripts remain strictly private.
